## Main idea

**Can AI detect defects it has never seen before?**

In industrial manufacturing, detecting defective products is crucial for quality control. However,
collecting representative examples of every possible defect is often difficult, so with this project
we aim to investigate whether models can learn the characteristics of <ins>correctly</ins> manufactured products
and subsequently identify defects they have never encountered during training.

So, instead of training models to recognize specific defects, we aim to train them primarily
using defect-free, correct images of products, allowing them to identify deviations from normality.

## Research Question

Our main research question is:

**How effectively can models trained exclusively on defect-free images detect and localize
previously unseen industrial defects?**

Our primary objective is image-level anomaly detection, meaning that the model should determine whether a product image represents a correctly manufactured product or contains a potential defect.

Additionally, we hope to explore anomaly localization, which involves identifying the specific regions of an image that contain defects and could provide further insights into how the models recognize abnormalities which is very interesting.

## Dataset

We plan to use the [MVTec Anomaly Detection (MVTec AD) dataset](https://www.mvtec.com/research-teaching/datasets/mvtec-ad).

The dataset contains industrial object and texture images, including defect-free training examples, normal and defective test examples, and pixel-level annotations for defective regions.

## First Thought Methodology

1. **Reconstruction-Based Anomaly Detection**

We will at first implement a Convolutional Autoencoder trained exclusively on defect-free images.

The model will learn to reconstruct normal products. At inference time, differences between an input image
and its reconstruction will be used to estimate potential anomalies.

2. **Feature-Based Anomaly Detection**

We will then investigate anomaly detection using feature representations extracted from a pretrained CNN.
By comparing image features with representations of normal training examples, we aim to identify unusual regions
that differ from the learned normal patterns.

## Experimental Evaluation

We plan to evaluate and compare the proposed models using both quantitative metrics and visual inspection.

Our evaluation will focus on two tasks:

1. **Anomaly Detection:** Determining whether an image contains a defective product.
2. **Anomaly Localization:** Identifying the specific regions of an image that contain defects.

For quantitative evaluation, we plan to use the **Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUROC)**, which measures how effectively a model distinguishes normal from anomalous observations across different classification thresholds.

We will consider:

- **Image-level AUROC:** To evaluate how accurately the model distinguishes normal and defective product images.
- **Pixel-level AUROC:** To evaluate how accurately the model distinguishes defective from normal pixels.

Additionally, we will visually compare the anomaly heatmaps generated by the models with the ground-truth defect masks provided by the dataset.

We also intend to investigate the influence of training dataset size and defect characteristics on model performance.
